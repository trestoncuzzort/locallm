arXiv:2411.00640 — Evan Miller, "Adding Error Bars to Evals"
  THE most relevant eval-statistics source anyone has cited into this project.
  Cited for: (1) the five recommendations — CLT standard errors; clustered SEs for
  grouped questions; variance reduction by resampling answers and by analysing
  next-token probabilities; "When two models are being compared, conducting
  statistical inference on the question-level paired differences, rather than the
  population-level summary statistics"; and using power analysis to determine
  whether an eval can test the hypothesis at all. (2) The planning formula
      n = (z_{alpha/2} + z_beta)^2 * (omega^2 + sigma_A^2/K_A + sigma_B^2/K_B)
          / delta^2,   omega^2 = Var(x_A) + Var(x_B) - 2*Cov(x_A, x_B)
  which carries PER-ARM conditional variances and per-arm resampling counts.
  (3) The paired-difference SE and the variance reduction
  Var(paired) = Var(unpaired) - 2*Cov(x_A, x_B)/n.
  Status: VERIFIED (HTML v1 fetched and read; the PDF is unreadable binary through
  the fetch tool, so the HTML is the source of record)
  APPLIED, directly and decisively — this is the whole Q2 answer:
    (a) The literature does NOT assume homoscedasticity. se_diff = sqrt(2)*run_sd
        is Miller's formula with sigma_A = sigma_B and K_A = K_B substituted in.
    (b) omega^2 IS the tau^2/T heterogeneity term §59 finding 2 named without a
        citation. Now cited. It also CONFIRMS that the project's own formula is
        correct for the estimand it declares: with 31 FIXED tasks and no
        generalisation claim, omega^2 = 0 and se_diff = sqrt(sd_A^2/k_A +
        sd_B^2/k_B) is exactly right — so pairing across tasks buys NO variance
        reduction here and the PhD explicitly declined to recommend it as one.
    (c) Drives the Q2(d) recommendation: each arm reports its own sd, Welch
        rather than pooled. Drives R7's reallocation.

arXiv:2501.18101 — "Diverse Preference Optimization" (DivPO)
  Cited for, verbatim: "Post-training of language models, either through
  reinforcement learning, preference optimization or supervised finetuning, tends
  to sharpen the output probability distribution and reduce the diversity of
  generated responses."
  Status: VERIFIED (abstract fetched, quote verbatim)
  APPLIED: the documented DIRECTION for Q2 — sharpening lowers per-task p(1-p),

  This is the false-positive mechanism the greedy/sampled split is now recommended
  as the diagnostic for.

PubMed 10750058 — Kieser & Friede, "Re-calculating the sample size in internal
  pilot study designs with control of the type I error rate", Statistics in
  Medicine, 2000
  Cited for: the internal-pilot design (re-estimate the variance at an interim
  look, then re-size) is a standard accepted answer to "I must size on a variance
  I can only measure after starting"; AND that naive re-estimation inflates alpha
  — "it also turned out that the type I error rate may be inflated by this
  procedure" — with strict control recovered via an adjusted critical value or a
