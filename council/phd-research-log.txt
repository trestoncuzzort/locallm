arXiv:2410.05605 — CodeDPO
  Cited for: data-scaling ablation (Table 7, DeepSeekCoder-1.3B). First 25% of
  data (~28,500 pairs) buys +1.4pp HumanEval pass@1; 114k pairs total.
  Status: VERIFIED (abs + HTML v1, Table 7 read directly)
  APPLIED: the single most decisive number behind the executor's Q1 effect-
  size target. Directly produced the "0 to +2pp, centred +0.5" prediction
  that §61's measured -0.8pp held-out result was checked against ("brackets
  the measured -0.8"). Re-cited in §62/§63 as the basis for ruling out a
  200-prompt sweep as premature until the seam is fixed.

arXiv:2411.13611 — DSTC
  Cited for: 50k self-generated-test instructions, DPO one epoch, full FT,
  StarCoder2-15B / DeepSeek-Coder-33B. +1.0 to +4.3pp across HumanEval+/MBPP+/
  BigCodeBench-hard.
  Status: VERIFIED (abs + HTML v2, Tables 2-3)
  APPLIED: paired with CodeDPO to frame Q2's "threshold between 13 and 28,500"
  question and to size the 200-prompt recommendation in §62 (deferred, not
  adopted — see NOT ADOPTED in §63).

arXiv:2406.06887 — PLUM
  Cited for: "+up to 4.8% average, +11.8% LiveCodeBench" (abstract verbatim).
  PLUM-DPO sometimes underperforms PLUM-KTO (DPO reducing chosen-class reward).
  Status: VERIFIED (abstract only; training config — LoRA vs full FT, epoch
  count — NOT extractable from HTML, flagged as partially unverified)
  APPLIED: NOT directly actioned this round. Its DPO-vs-KTO instability
  finding sits behind the still-open §58 item 2 (DPO/SFT/KTO admissibility)
  and Ruling R5's rpo_alpha ablation, but no executor decision cites it by
  name yet.

arXiv:2502.01718 — ACECODER
  Cited for: RL arm (Reinforce++, test-case reward) results, +1.8/+3.7pp on
  Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct; -2.5pp regression on Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct. Also the
  source of the AceCode-89K dataset construction / stratum seed counts (evol
  36,256→26,920, oss 37,750→25,862, StackPyFunction 50,000→34,367 = 87,149).
  Status: VERIFIED (abs + HTML v3, dataset-construction + RL sections)
  APPLIED, twice, directly:
    (1) Its seed-count table is the exact arithmetic identity (sums to
        87,149) that resolved the evol-stratum-mapping question — this is
        what let the executor change evol's recorded status from
        "provenance unverified" to "verified Apache-2.0."
    (2) Its earlier (pre-council) citation by the executor as an optimistic
        effect-size comparable was corrected by the PhD — ACECODER's +10/+5pp
        numbers come from best-of-32 + a trained reward model, NOT this
        project's plain-DPO setup — removing it as false comfort for Q1's
        sizing.

arXiv:2405.09673 — LoRA Learns Less and Forgets Less (Biderman et al.)

arXiv:2406.01462 — Song, Swamy, Singh, Bagnell, Sun — "The Importance of
  Online Data: Understanding Preference Fine-tuning via Coverage"
  Cited for: offline DPO needs GLOBAL coverage of the preference dataset
  (necessary+sufficient); online RL only needs weaker partial coverage. The
  key citation making off-policy-ness a coverage/prompt-diversity problem.
  Status: VERIFIED (abs + rev.)
  APPLIED, directly: this is the citation that answered the DIRECTIVE's Q1
  ranking question — off-policy-ness is real but SECOND-ORDER behind prompt
  count/coverage. That ranking is the stated justification behind Ruling R2
  in §63 (hold prompt count at 13, don't move two variables at once) and
  behind treating the format-seam fix (R1) as higher priority than a
  quantization-driven pipeline rebuild.

arXiv:2404.14367 — Tajwar, Singh, Sharma, Rafailov, Schneider, Xie, Ermon,
  Finn, Kumar — "Preference Fine-Tuning of LLMs Should Leverage Suboptimal,
  On-Policy Data" (ICML 2024)
  Cited for: on-policy sampling OR a negative gradient (DPO has the latter)
  outperforms offline/MLE objectives — argues off-policy gap is real but
  second-order for DPO specifically.
  Status: VERIFIED (abstract, verbatim quote used)
  APPLIED: co-supporting evidence for the same R1/R2 prioritization above —
  DPO already has the "negative gradient" property the paper says matters
  most, so the on-policy gap was ranked as the smaller of the two problems.

arXiv:2404.10719 — Xu, Fu, Gao, Ye, Liu, Mei, Wang, Yu, Wu — "Is DPO Superior
  to PPO for LLM Alignment? A Comprehensive Study" (ICML 2024)
  Cited for: Theorem 4.1 — DPO's solution set is strictly larger than PPO's
  and can exploit OOD data (worked example: DPO hands 0.9 probability to an
  unseen third action; empirical OOD probability rise 0.11→0.23).
  Status: VERIFIED (abs + HTML v3, Theorem 4.1 + example read directly)
  APPLIED, directly: cited as the theoretical reason 918 pairs over 13
  prompts is "close to their degenerate illustration" — this is the specific
  argument behind Ruling R5 (rpo_alpha=1.0 recorded as a live confound
  needing an ablation arm at rpo_alpha=0, still pending as of §64).

arXiv:2508.18312 — Pan, Cai, Chen, Zhong, Wang — "What Matters in Data for
  DPO?"
  Cited for: chosen-response quality dominates the DPO objective; online DPO
  "effectively reduces to supervised fine-tuning on the chosen responses" —
  predicts the srlm-forge held-in/held-out split mechanically.
  Status: VERIFIED (abstract, verbatim quote used)
  APPLIED, directly: measured against data/dpo_pairs_capped.jsonl (407
  distinct chosen strings over 13 prompts) to argue Round Zero was
  "approximately SFT on 407 specific strings." This is the direct evidentiary
  basis for Ruling R5 — the executor recorded it as meaning "13 prompts of
  DPO does not transfer" may actually be "13 prompts of SFT-with-a-margin-
  term does not transfer," unresolved until the rpo_alpha=0 ablation runs.

arXiv:2604.07853 — Gu et al. — "QaRL: Rollout-Aligned Quantization-Aware RL
  for Fast and Stable Training under Training-Inference Mismatch"
  Cited for: quantized-rollout / full-precision-update mismatch destabilises
  ONLINE RL optimization (garbled-token failure mode); +5.5 on Qwen3-30B-A3B
  math fixing it. Argued NOT to transfer to offline DPO (no importance ratio).
  Status: VERIFIED (abstract)
  APPLIED: used to answer DIRECTIVE Q1 part B in the negative — this
  mechanism doesn't apply to srlm-forge's offline DPO (π_ref and π_θ are the
  same bnb-4bit artifact, no importance ratio in the loss). This is why the
  executor folded "regenerate pairs from the trained artifact" into the
  single R1 seam fix instead of treating quantization as its own urgent,
  separate rebuild.

arXiv:2605.14220 — Zhong et al. — "Diagnosing Training Inference Mismatch in
  LLM Reinforcement Learning"
  Cited for: Training-Inference Mismatch (TIM) framework (VeXact); small
  token-level disagreements can independently cause training collapse, in RL.
  Status: VERIFIED (abstract)
  APPLIED: same role as Gu et al. above — confirms the mechanism is real but
  RL-specific, reinforcing the decision not to treat quantization mismatch
  as the priority fix.

arXiv:2407.09141 — Dutta, Krishnan, Kwatra, Ramjee — "Accuracy is Not All You
  Need" (NeurIPS 2024)
  Cited for: "flips" phenomenon — per-task correct/incorrect flips between
  baseline and compressed models at matched aggregate accuracy. Names the
  6-of-13 per-task engine disagreement observed in srlm-forge.
  Status: PARTIALLY VERIFIED — existence/definition of "flips" confirmed from
  abstract; numeric flip-rate magnitudes UNVERIFIED (HTML v3 404s, PDF
  unreadable binary) — do not use for magnitude comparison
  APPLIED: framing only, no numeric action — gave the executor's observed
  6-of-13 disagreement a name and a citation, but explicitly could NOT be
  used to judge whether that rate is high or normal, so it did not change any
  ruling by itself.

arXiv:2504.20571 — Wang, Yang, Zeng, Ren et al. — "Reinforcement Learning for
  Reasoning in Large Language Models with One Training Example"
  Cited for: ONE training example via on-policy RLVR (GRPO/PPO) moves
  Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B 36.0%→73.6% MATH500 and 17.6%→35.7% averaged across six
  OOD reasoning benchmarks. The single most destabilizing citation this
  round — suggests method (on-policy + verifiable reward), not prompt count,
  is the real variable.
  Status: VERIFIED (abstract)
  APPLIED: NOT YET ACTED ON, deliberately. §63 records it verbatim as "the
  most destabilising citation in the round" and "the most important open
  question in the project" but explicitly declines to fold it into a plan
  this round, pending the seam fix and ruler replacement being resolved
  first.

arXiv:2504.13837 — Yue et al. — "Does Reinforcement Learning Really
  Incentivize Reasoning Capacity in LLMs Beyond the Base Model?"
  Cited for: counter-argument — RLVR raises pass@1 while lowering pass@k at
  large k; capability is "bounded by the base model," not fundamentally new.
  Used to explain the srlm-forge held-out bank being saturated (base pass@20
  = pass@3 = 0.90, per §64).
  Status: VERIFIED (abstract, v5)
  APPLIED, directly and concretely: this is the interpretive frame the
  executor used in §64 for the base-model pass@k measurement (pass@20 =
  pass@5 = pass@3 = 0.9000 exactly on the held-out bank). It reframed §61's
  held-out null from "evidence training didn't work" to "the ruler had only
  3 points of headroom to show it in" — directly motivating Ruling R3/R4
  (restart the screen, replace the ruler from a fresh oss draw rather than
  reusing the saturated admitted-30 set).

arXiv:2210.11416 — Chung et al. — "Scaling Instruction-Finetuned Language
  Models" (Flan)
  Cited for: the only real published task-count learning curve (PaLM 540B,
  Table 3): 9 tasks→+3.5, 89→+7.9 (84% of max gain), 282→+8.4, 1836→+9.4.
  Heavily caveated: a Flan "task" is a whole dataset (thousands of prompts
  each) — srlm-forge's 13 prompts sits ~3 orders of magnitude below Flan's
  smallest measured point. Shape-only evidence, not a transferable number.
  Status: VERIFIED (HTML via ar5iv, Table 3 read directly)
  APPLIED: sole basis for the numeric "target 200 distinct prompts"
  recommendation in §62 (low-moderate confidence, explicitly called an
  off-domain interpolation, not a measurement). Executor's §63 ruling: NOT
  ADOPTED yet — deferred, not rejected, pending the seam fix (R1) and ruler
  replacement (R3/R4) landing first, specifically to avoid ratifying DPO "by
  accident" per the sponsor's sheet-ordering rule.
