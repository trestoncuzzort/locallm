Full Advisor Responses

The Contrarian

Option (a) is disqualified as written, not ranked. rotate and rle are ruler tasks — mining them is training on the test set laundered through "self-lane"; the mined prompts ARE eval prompts, so "ruler delta > 0.02" would measure contamination, and it violates the collision-filter spec by name. The executor conflated reachable with harvestable. Salvage as (a′): harvest the training bank only.

The success bar is independently broken: cross-run std 0.009 over 3 runs is test-retest repeatability of a near-deterministic eval, not between-model variance; with 8/10 tasks at ceiling, Δ>0.02 is one-two samples; the "<4/5" guard trips ~8% by chance per 0.9-rate task — the prereg can fail on a no-op.

(b) repeats the rotate mistake: n=100 cannot "confirm 0-mass" — a true-1% task yields 0/100 ~37% of the time, and 0/100's Wilson upper (~3.6%) overlaps rotate's band. Reframe as "unreachable at harvest budget B." And the ~60-pair stockpile accrued unattended with the filter as spec, not verified code — audit before ORPO. Ranking: audit, (a′), reframed (b). Transfer: defer.

The First Principles Thinker

You cannot "harvest rotate/rle self-pairs" — those are ruler tasks; any ruler movement afterward is memorization, not evidence. What the first run actually tests, now that injection is moot, is the core thesis: bank-mined verifier-grounded pairs moving a genuinely held-out ruler via generalization. The legitimate run is (a′): one ORPO epoch on stockpiled bank pairs plus high-K bank mining for dose.

Don't burn rotate. A principled retirement exists (pre-registered swap, replacement re-baselined before training) but it spends the most sensitive detector: at 1/100, rotate sits exactly where per-task binomial movement is detectable; rle and the aggregate are ceiling-compressed and can mostly only move down.

1/100 vs 0-mass fixes transfer's place: labeled distillation's honest home is genuinely 0-mass tasks — known_hard. Prereg per-task, not aggregate: rotate ≥ ~10/100; secondary aggregate within 2σ. And "no regression" is a positive first-run result — the null for tiny-dose ORPO on q4 is collapse; a flat ruler with an intact model validates the infrastructure and licenses dose escalation. Rank: (a′) first, (b) concurrent, (a)-as-written never.

The Expansionist

(a) is flywheel ignition — decisively first, with (b) as a parallel background confirm. The first epoch answers the only question that matters: does self-generated preference signal move a frozen ruler? The compounding is nonlinear: rotate at p=0.01 → best-of-12 lands ~11%; at p=0.05 → ~46%. Small ruler deltas become multiplicative mining-yield gains — each harvest round gets cheaper as the checkpoint improves. Cadence: harvest → train → re-measure → re-mine where eval_history says the frontier warmed.

Transfer just got demoted, which is the best news of the night: "impossible" mostly meant underpowered sampling. Its new role is precision instrument for the true 0-mass residue — and known_hard.json is auto-curating that target list; whatever survives quarantine is the honest transfer experiment, pre-registered by the pipeline itself.

The unpriced asset: the frontier ledger — per-task eval_history + known_hard.json + git-per-round is a self-documenting record of a model eating its own frontier; family-expanding the bank scales the self-lane horizontally with zero distillation risk.

The Outsider

Option (a) breaks the one rule the whole setup depends on. The exam's value is "never trained on"; the moment rotate and rle enter training data, the exam measures the leak, not the model. The success criterion rigs itself: two exam questions are worth 20 points, the bar is 2 — train on exact exam items and a >2-point move is near-guaranteed and certifies nothing. And it's irreversible: you can't un-leak an exam — it's dead for all future runs.

The 1/100 rotate success is a lottery ticket, not competence — training on a single lucky output risks teaching it to reproduce that one string. rle at 46/50 is essentially solved; training on it buys almost nothing. So (a) combines a contaminated metric with weak training material.

What convinces a stranger: keep the exam sacred; train on FRESH rotate-like and rle-like tasks written for training, then check whether the untouched exam's rotate climbs from 1/100 toward 30/100. Ranking: (b) first (cheap, honest — and a true 0/100 task is still worth finding, because retries can't amplify zero); then repaired (a) on non-exam tasks; (a) as written: never.
